Magic & Superpower Hackathon

A build-first hackathon where students turn magical artifacts, superpowers, sci-fi gadgets, AI sidekicks, and fictional interfaces into real working prototypes.

✨ Explore Prompts 📜 Read Concept

Why — Builder Confidence

Most student hackathons push participants toward problem statements, solution pitches, market logic, and startup-style judging. That creates unnecessary pressure for beginners. Fiction to Function removes that pressure completely.

Students do not need to solve a serious problem in order to learn how to build. The intended outcome is foundational — students should leave thinking, "I can make weird ideas real."

"The permission structure — Students are allowed to build playful, impractical, or theatrical things because the act of building is itself valuable."

What This Is Not

To keep the center of gravity on playful experimentation, we have explicit anti-goals.

  • This is not a startup hackathon.
  • This is not a problem-statement hackathon.
  • This is not a competition where the best project is the most practical project.

The best project is the one that best turns a fictional idea into a working, memorable demo.

What You Can Build

The theme is speculative fiction, including magic, science fiction, fantasy, superheroes, futuristic interfaces, and impossible-feeling machines.

Take something that feels like it belongs in fiction and make a working prototype. Teams can build a talking painting that responds to visitors, a fake Jarvis that gives dramatic assistance, a spellbook interface for triggering automations, or a wearable that gives silly "powers" based on motion.

Safety is a Hard Boundary

The theme includes magic and fictional devices, but every build must be safe for participants, audiences, and venues.

The rules explicitly exclude weapons, harmful projectiles, fire, explosives, electric shock devices, or builds that invade privacy without consent. Anything that creates real physical danger while trying to imitate fiction is disqualified.

"Build the feeling of the fictional idea, not the dangerous part of it."

The Build Festival Format

This should feel closer to a high-energy build festival than a competitive hackathon. We start with a showcase of playful builds, form teams around shared moments of wonder, and dedicate time to building with mentors.

A midpoint demo check matters because the event is built around live proof. Teams are pushed to finish something observable, even if it is small, culminating in a theatrical live showcase.

  1. 1 Inspire (Playful showcase)
  2. 2 Choose (Fictional prompts)
  3. 3 Build (Low pressure)
  4. 4 Check (Midpoint demo focus)
  5. 5 Demo (Live theatrical proof)

Judging Wonder Before Utility

Judging criteria intentionally avoid startup-style measures such as market size, problem validation, scalability, or social impact. We reward wonder, craft, demo quality, and learning. Usefulness is welcome, but optional.
