# Inclusivity and Accessibility

Inclusivity and accessibility are part of the hackathon design, not icing on top of the cake. They should shape the invitation, the examples, the team formation, the venue, the tools, the build process, and the final demo.

A build festival works best when more people can see themselves in the ideas, participate in the making, and show what they built in ways that work for them. The goal is not to create a separate experience for a few participants; it is to make the main experience welcoming and usable from the beginning.

## Design Commitments

- Make deliberate space for equal participation across genders.
- Choose example prompts that offer many kinds of excitement, including adventures, transformations, mysteries, magic, collaboration, and imaginative worlds.
- Include a wide range of fictional inspirations, and invite participants to bring characters and worlds they already love.
- Use clear, readable instructions and explain jargon before asking beginners to build.
- Make participation possible through speech, typing, drawing, movement, or a quiet hands-on role; no single body, voice, or confidence style should be required.
- Provide accessible routes, seating, power, and rest areas, and ask participants privately what accommodations would help them take part.
- Caption spoken demos where possible, describe visual effects aloud, and offer a written or recorded version of important instructions.
- Design prompts and judging so that originality, collaboration, learning, and a working idea matter more than volume, speed, expensive hardware, or stage confidence.
- Establish a respectful code of conduct, clear reporting routes, and a facilitator who can intervene early when someone is excluded or interrupted.

## Inclusive Prompt Examples

The prompt list now includes a wider range of superhero and magic references so that participants can find a starting point that feels exciting to them:

- **Wonder Woman's Truth Interface:** Build a consent-based question-and-answer installation that turns honest answers into a visual constellation; participants can answer by voice, text, or buttons.
- **Ms. Marvel's Stretchy Interface:** Build a playful interface that expands, rearranges, and adapts when a user changes the screen size, input method, or accessibility preference.
- **Captain Marvel's Cosmic Navigator:** Build a voice-friendly star map that lets a team explore fictional destinations using spoken commands, keyboard controls, or large touch targets.
- **Zatanna's Reverse Spellbook:** Build a spellbook that transforms words typed forwards or backwards into lights, sounds, animations, or a small digital action.
- **Sailor Moon Transformation Console:** Build a personal, non-competitive transformation sequence with captions, sound controls, visual effects, and a choice of character or avatar.
- **The Princess Alliance Mission Board:** Build a collaborative mission board where each teammate can choose a role—builder, storyteller, tester, designer, researcher, or presenter—and contribute asynchronously.
- **Enchanted Garden Caretaker:** Build a magical garden that responds to gentle touch, keyboard input, or voice and gives every visitor a different low-pressure creative challenge.

These are starting points, not assignments. Participants should be free to remix them, choose a different character, or invent a new world entirely.

## Accessibility Is Built In

For every activity, ask:

1. Can participants understand the instructions in more than one format?
2. Can they contribute without needing a particular physical movement, speaking style, device, or prior experience?
3. Can the demo be experienced through more than one sense?
4. Can the team explain what they learned even if the prototype is incomplete?
5. Does the room, schedule, and judging format leave enough space for different access needs?

When the answer is no, change the design before the event begins. Accessibility is not a bonus feature added after the main experience; it is part of making the hackathon work.

## Participant Promise

You belong here whether your strength is code, electronics, storytelling, design, testing, organizing, or simply asking a great question. Bring an idea that excites you, help make room for other people’s ideas, and build a version of the magic that more people can experience.
